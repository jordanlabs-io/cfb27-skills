# Fourth and Film — Every Running Concept Explained — Visual Pass

Same whiteboard/motion-graphic diagram style as the down-and-distance video: full
11-personnel formation card, color-coded positions (QB=black, RB=red, FB=orange,
OL=blue, TE=pink/magenta, WR=yellow, defense=white), a bold concept-name title in
a small top-left thumbnail box showing an X's/O's route sketch, and a **red arrow
tracing the ball carrier's path** through the formation. On several blocking-heavy
concepts (Power O, Counter, Trap, Dart) a second **yellow arrow traces the pulling
lineman's path** — this is the field most directly reusable for our
`run_direction`/blocking charting.

Three sub-families covered, in order: **man concepts** (0:20–5:10), **zone
concepts** (6:15–16:36), **gap concepts** (16:40–end).

## Man concepts

- **Lead Dive** [00:50–02:10]: FB leads through the hole with a down/kickout-style
  double team ahead of him; red RB arrow runs straight downhill behind the FB.
  Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-0055-lead-dive.jpg`.
- **Isolation (ISO)** [02:10–03:50]: Similar to lead dive but the diagram isolates
  a single defender (usually a LB) with the FB as the lead blocker aimed directly
  at him — no double team drawn, just one lineman-on-defender iso block.
  Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-0218-isolation.jpg`.
- **Man Dive** [04:00–04:54]: Not separately captured in this pass — visually
  close to Lead Dive per narration (no FB, single hole).
- **Man Toss** [04:54–06:15]: Not separately captured in this pass — outside run
  with pulling-style reach blocks per narration.

## Zone concepts

- **Inside Zone** [08:35–11:47]: Thumbnail labeled "Inside Zone"; linemen shown at
  their normal alignment (no pulling arrows) with a red RB arrow curving from the
  backfield through a single interior gap. Diagnostic tell: **no yellow pulling
  arrow, red RB path bends laterally before cutting up.**
  Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-0842-inside-zone.jpg`.
- **Split Zone** [12:01–12:28]: Same inside-zone OL picture, but adds a blocker
  (FB/TE) crossing the formation opposite the play's zone flow (the "split"
  element) — captured near this timestamp but re-verify the crossing blocker is
  visible in-frame before citing. Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-1208-split-zone.jpg`.
- **Zone Read** [12:28–13:07]: Not separately captured — same OL picture as
  inside zone, per narration the only difference is QB read/keep, not a new
  blocking diagram.
- **Mid Zone** [13:07–13:33]: Not separately captured — narration-only variant
  (RB aiming just outside tackle's hip vs. inside zone's inside-leg aim point).
- **Outside Zone** [13:33–15:29]: RB/red-arrow path noticeably wider/flatter than
  inside zone, aiming for the edge with a pronounced lateral "stretch" bend before
  any upfield cut. Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-1338-outside-zone.jpg`.
- **Wide Zone** [15:29–15:53]: Named as a variant of outside zone with even more
  stretch emphasis — not separately captured, same diagram family as outside zone.
- **Jet Zone** [15:53–16:36]: Adds a receiver in jet motion crossing pre-snap
  behind the OL, combined with inside-zone blocking — not separately captured in
  this pass; distinguishing visual would be the extra WR motion arrow across the
  formation.

## Gap concepts

- **Power O** [16:42–19:23]: The single clearest blocking diagram in the video.
  Guard+tackle double team the inside DT, another lineman kicks out the DE, and a
  **yellow arrow shows the backside guard pulling** through the C gap ahead of the
  red RB arrow. Diagnostic tell: **one pulling yellow arrow originating from the
  backside guard position.** Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-1650-power-o-pulling-guard.jpg`.
- **Counter** [19:51–20:25]: Same double-team picture as Power O, but **two**
  pulling elements — backside guard (kickout) and backside tackle (wraparound
  lead through the C gap) — plus the RB shows an initial jab step backside before
  redirecting. Diagnostic tell: **two pullers instead of one.**
  Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-1956-counter-two-pullers.jpg`.
- **Duo** [20:25–22:35]: No pulling linemen at all — the diagnostic tell versus
  Power/Counter. Two adjacent double teams at the point of attack (center-guard on
  the nose, guard-tackle on the 3-tech) with the TE always on the play side; red
  RB arrow is short/vertical, reading the Mike backer rather than following a
  puller. Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-2030-duo-double-teams.jpg`.
- **Trap** [22:48–25:03]: Interior DT shown penetrating upfield unblocked
  initially (bait), then a **yellow arrow shows the backside guard pulling flat
  behind the center** to block him from the side; red RB arrow cuts sharply
  behind that trap block. Diagnostic tell: **puller crosses the formation
  laterally low/flat rather than wrapping upfield like Power's puller.**
  Frame: `Of_TwCsCETs-2253-trap-bait-block.jpg`.
- **Wham** [25:03–25:29]: Same trap concept, but the surprise blocker is a
  TE/H-back from the backfield rather than a pulling guard — not separately
  captured; distinguishing visual would be the extra TE/FB box positioned in the
  backfield pre-snap instead of on the line.
- **G Lead** [25:29–26:04], **T Lead** [26:04–26:19]: Guard or tackle pulls
  through the C gap as the lead blocker (not a kickout) targeting the playside
  LB directly, rest of the line walls off inside — not separately captured in
  this pass.
- **Dart** [26:19–26:43]: Power-like double teams, but the **backside tackle**
  (not guard) pulls and leads through the playside B gap. Frame:
  `Of_TwCsCETs-2624-dart-backside-tackle-pull.jpg`.
- **Speed Option** [26:43–end (video ends ~27:00)]: Edge-attacking option look
  with aggressive reach/seal blocking; not captured as a distinct diagram — video
  ends shortly after introducing it (no Read Option comparison frame reached).

## Notes for charting agents

- **Pulling-lineman count and path shape is the primary discriminator for gap
  runs**: 0 pullers = Duo, 1 puller wrapping upfield = Power O, 2 pullers = Counter,
  1 puller flat/lateral behind the formation = Trap/Wham, tackle (not guard)
  pulling = Dart.
- **Zone runs never show a pulling arrow** — only the red RB path shape changes
  (tight vertical bend = Inside Zone, wide flat stretch = Outside/Wide Zone).
  This is the fastest zone-vs-gap visual triage rule.
- Every concept's mini top-left thumbnail names the play and sketches its route
  in miniature X's/O's — same reusable template as the down-and-distance video's
  corner thumbnails.
