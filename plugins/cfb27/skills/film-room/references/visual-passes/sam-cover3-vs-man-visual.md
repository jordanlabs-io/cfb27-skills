## Doubles look — using motion to distinguish Cover 3 vs Cover 1
- [00:00:22]-[00:01:00]: pre-snap picture, Clemson offense in a "doubles" set (2 receivers to one side, a receiver + tight end to the other). One deep safety visible in the middle of the field, a defender (nickel) walked out over the 2-receiver side — this is the ambiguous "could be either coverage" picture he references.
- [00:01:49]-[00:02:14]: after he sends a receiver in motion across the formation, the camera shows the nickel defender running across with him (following the motion man), while the single safety stays high/deep in the middle of the field rather than rotating down — this is the visual proof point he calls out: "the safety went up top" confirms zone (Cover 3), because a true man safety could never align 20 yards deep over a receiver.
- [00:02:55]-[00:03:04]: post-motion set frame — nickel now aligned to the new passing-strength side (the side with more receivers/motion man), safety still deep middle — visually confirms the coverage "exchanged responsibilities" rather than the safety traveling with the motion man.

## Trips look — alignment alone reveals coverage (no motion needed)
- [00:05:36]-[00:06:15] (~C_340 zoom frame at 00:05:40): pre-snap picture now shows a 3-receiver (trips) side, with the nickel defender and a safety BOTH visible on the trips side of the formation, and only a single defender (corner) alone on the backside/tight-end side — this is the exact tell he describes: "the nickel and the safety are on the same side of the formation" = Cover 3 (their landmarks/help rules put them there); had it been Cover 1, the safety would instead be aligned over the backside tight end in a straight man matchup.
- [00:07:06]-[00:08:15]: he cross-references the same trips picture, pointing out the backside safety flying across late (matching the tight end in man-look) as the contrasting Cover 1 picture — a different rep where the safety rotates/travels with a tight end in motion rather than staying attached to trips-side landmarks, used purely as a spoken contrast rather than a fresh diagrammed rep.

## Visual tells summarized (as explicitly called out on screen)
1. **Doubles formations are ambiguous pre-snap** — nickel and safety can appear on opposite sides in both Cover 1 and Cover 3, so a single static screenshot can't disambiguate them.
2. **Motion is the diagnostic tool**: sending a receiver in motion and watching whether the safety stays deep (zone/Cover 3) or travels with a receiver (man/Cover 1) is the reliable visual read — captured in the doubles-motion sequence above.
3. **Trips formations self-reveal**: because Cover 3's nickel-and-safety-on-the-same-side rule is a hard constraint, a trips picture alone (no motion needed) tells you the coverage — nickel+safety together on the trips side = Cover 3; safety opposite the nickel (over the backside receiver/TE) = Cover 1.
4. No in-game zone-landmark diagram overlay (the DEEP ZONE/HOOK-CURL bubbles seen in videos A and B) appears in this video — all teaching here is done through live pre/post-snap alignment shots only, no coach-cam diagram screen.
