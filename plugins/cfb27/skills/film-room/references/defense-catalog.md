## 2. Coverages

Condensed from Mills (alignment / soft spots / ideal usage):

| Coverage | Alignment definition | Soft spots (cons) | Ideal usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cover 1 | 1 deep safety; hole = LB in hook/robber, S in hook; man outside leverage | Deep outside fades 1-on-1; backs turned (scrambles); FS is last line | Standard downs; vs crossing-route teams |
| Cover 3 Sky | SS rolls to flat/curl, CB deep 1/3, FS deep middle 1/3 | Seams (hash marks); soft flats if S rolls late | Base 1st & 10; running downs |
| Cover 3 Cloud | CB stays shallow in flat (trap), S rolls over top deep 1/3 — Cover 2 look on one side | Edge run support weaker (CB must tackle); rotates away from strength | 2nd & long; sideline passing teams |
| Cover 3 Buzz | S drops into the hook (middle), LBs push out to flats, CBs deep 1/3 | Weak in flats (LBs sprint to edge); needs athletic LBs | 3rd & medium; disguise |
| Cover 3 Match | Seam defenders carry verticals; converts to man deep; CBs "man on deep" | High bust potential; motion/shifts confuse match rules | Modern standard defense vs spread |
| Cover 2 | 2 deep halves, 2 squat corners, 3 LBs hook/curl | Turkey hole (deep sideline CB/S gap); open deep middle (posts); 7-man box vs run | 2nd & long; bend-don't-break |
| Cover 2 Man | 2 deep halves, 5 underneath pure man (trail technique) | QB scramble (backs turned); very light box (draws/screens) | 3rd & long; 2-minute; protecting a lead |
| Cover 2 Invert | CB bails to deep half, S rolls down to flat/run fit — looks like C3, flips at snap | CBs not natural deep-half players; deep outs if QB recognizes | Change-up vs run-heavy; disguise |
| Cover 4 Quarters | 4 deep quarter defenders; safeties read #2 | Play action freezes safeties; posts isolate safeties 1-on-1 | Base vs spread / RPO |
| Cover 4 Palms | S/CB read #2: out → CB traps it; up → plays Quarters | Sluggo / double moves can be TDs | vs bubble-screen / quick-game teams |
| Cover 6 | Quarter-Quarter-Half; field = Quarters vs 2x2 / Palms vs 3x1 (Box); boundary = Cover 2 (Triangle) | Complex communication (low play rec busts); boundary S covers lots of ground | vs trips; field/boundary hashes |
| Cover 9 | Half-Quarter-Quarter; field = Cover 2 (Triangle), boundary = Quarters (Box) | Same as Cover 6 | Matchup-specific (star WR to field) |
| Cover 0 | No deep safety, all man, usually 6+ rushers | Zero margin; picks; no deep help | Goal line; short yardage; must-stop |
| Fire Zone | 5 rush, 3 deep + 3 under; often drops a DL into shallow hole | Seams open quickly; unathletic DL droppers in space | Standard downs, force negative plays |
| Sim Pressure | Shows blitz, rushes only 4, returns to 2-high or 3-deep shell | Weak vs run (LBs backing out); if picked up it's a standard rush | 3rd & long; passing downs |

Post-patch (2026-08-11): **cover four zone / cover six zone / cover nine zone** exist as selectable pure-zone variants — pre-patch the "zone" toggle only zoned these out vs stack/bunch/trips, matching everything else. A charted match-looking C4/C6 vs doubles/spread may now legitimately be pure zone.

## 4. Stunts

Driver crashes first and occupies blockers; looper wraps behind into the vacated gap. On film, name from which ghost trails cross and where they land, then confirm vs menu tiles. A charted `dl_stunt_seen: twist` maps as follows:

| Stunt | Driver → Looper | Ghost-trail signature | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TEX** (L/R) | Tackle crashes B-gap → End loops under into A-gap | Edge trail bends INSIDE, crossing behind the DT's trail; edge contain vacated | vs pocket-steppers; inside zone |
| **EXIT** (L/R) | End crashes B-gap → Tackle loops outside to C/D | Interior trail bends OUTSIDE, crossing behind the end's crash | Contain rush; mobile QBs; outside zone |
| **TOM** (L/R) | 3-tech crashes A-gap → Nose loops to B-gap | Two interior trails cross each other; edges rush straight | 3rd & long; weak centers |
| **PIRATE** (L/R, 3-man) | End spikes B + Tackle spikes A → Nose/LB loops all the way to C-gap | Two down trails converge inside, one long looping trail wraps wide around both | vs slide protection; 3rd & long (sim pressure) |
| **Texas** (4-man) | Both Tackles crash B-gaps → both Ends loop inside to A-gaps | BOTH edge trails cross inside — no contain either side | Interior collapse vs non-mobile QBs |
| **El Paso** (4-man) | Both Ends crash B-gaps → both Tackles loop outside | Both interior trails cross outward — a "cage"/mush rush, soft middle | vs highly mobile QBs |
| **Tempe** (L/R) | Call side runs a Tex; away side slants away or runs a Tom | One-side Tex crossing, other side slant/interior twist — asymmetric | Standard pass rush; changes launch point |

Field usage note (creator meta): Texas Fourman is the community's inside-run killer, El Paso the outside-run answer; the 2026-08-07 patch nerfed their reliability against the run (unresolved creator split on how much) — chart the stunt seen, not its pre-patch reputation.

## 5. Disguise & post-snap machinery

Chart-time implication throughout: pre-snap alignment is allowed to lie; the tools below are why.

- **Coverage shells** (coaching adjustment): different underlying coverages present identically pre-snap. **Humans yes, CPU no** — CPU opponents in Dynasty/RTG do not use shells at all, so pre-snap reads stay reliable vs CPU film only. Human meta bases the disguise on cover 2 or cover 4 shells (2-high, consistent corner alignment); cover-3 shells are harder to sell.
- **Plaster**: zone defenders latch onto nearby receivers when the play breaks down. Settings: aggressiveness (off / conservative = backside only / aggressive = everyone) × trigger (timer, out-of-pocket, or both — default requires both; OOP fires the instant the QB leaves the pocket). "Look for work" is a separate smart-zone behavior (emptied zone defender hunts the next threat). Late man-looking latches on scramble plays = plaster, not man coverage.
- **Switch-stick**: mid-play user control transfer (flick toward another defender). Named variants: side switch (bait one flooded route, take the other), up switch (onto a safety vs MOF attacks), double switch (flat defender first, then the deep zone). A defender making an out-of-structure play is often the human, not the scheme — don't chart user plays as coverage tendencies.
- **Coverage roll** (coaching adjustment): defense rolls/drifts toward a designated target (fastest, highest-OVR, or WR slot) — zone defenders subtly cheat toward him even without a "roll" play call.
- **Cross-manning**: any defender (including DL) can be manned onto any receiver, opposite-side included — a defender stationed across the formation from "his" man is a legal assignment, not a bust; built as a crosser/drag counter.
- **Auto-flip**: on = alignment strength mirrors offensive strength automatically; off = numbers stay put (used deliberately for weak-side overloads). A defense misaligned to strength may be an intentional auto-flip-off blitz setup.
- **Deep-zone focus**: per-receiver adjustment biasing every deep-zone defender toward one receiver's deep routes — a safety rallying hard to one specific vert is targeted focus, not a coverage family tell.
- **Green dog**: user manned on the RB adds to the rush if the back stays to block (mugged looks; 3-creator convergence). A "late" extra rusher after the back blocks is green-dog logic.
- **Mug / hover protection manipulation**: mugged sub-LBs (Mug/Double/Single Mug fronts, §1) force protection checks then often bail — mugged alignment predicts pressure poorly. Related: "untargeting" — opponents key protection to where the user stands, so a switched-off user comes free; blitz origin can be user-driven, not scheme.
- **Zone strategy** (5 levels, per-zone or global) + shading (L2 + RS down/up maps underneath/deep defenders to ultra-aggressive/aggressive and the reverse): the same stock zone call plays visibly different depths/trigger speeds — depth behavior differences across reps of one call are settings, not different coverages.

## 6. Known engine bugs — NEVER chart as tendencies

**Failure-attribution rule: separate "beaten by scheme" from "beaten by engine."** A rep that matches a known bug signature gets logged as engine noise, not opponent tendency or coverage identity.

- **Motion breaks zone assignments (QB-matchups bug)**: with the quarterback-matchups adjustment set to anything but balanced (speed/height/route-running), zone defenders visibly flip/reassign wrongly on a simple motion — one-play-TD busts. A zone shell that self-destructs on motion is this bug, not a bad coverage call by the opponent.
- **Bunch/box check bug**: the universal quarters/palms bunch check "box" is broken vs Y-trail — the nickel fails to buy the flat-then-up move, "this guy's going to be running open all the time." A box-check defender losing Y-trail is engine, not a chartable soft spot the opponent schemed.
- **Block-shed issues**: pancake blocking (esp. pre-2026-08-07-patch WR-on-DB) and weak sheds mean a defender erased at the point of attack may be an animation loss, not a personnel or leverage failure; post-patch pancakes reduced but sheds still flagged "too bad" by one creator (unresolved).
- Also in the noise bucket: plaster mis-targeting (two defenders latching one receiver, another naked), intermittent switch-stick input drops, and a possible corner-width no-op (single-creator, unconfirmed). Single blown match reps generally: possible bug, never scheme identity on their own.

## 7. RPO & decoy mechanics

- **RPO read-key rule (hard-coded)**: every RPO designates a read-key defender (on-field "R", offense-side only — typically first defender outside the tackle, or the TE-side edge). **He never plays his zone/man assignment** — his whole job is to trigger give/keep/throw. Moving a different defender into that spot just relocates the key onto him. Film implication: a flat/edge defender who "never gets to his zone" against RPO looks isn't slow or busted — he's been read out; chart the OFFENSE's conflict-defender targeting, not a defensive weakness.
- **Conflict-defender logic (offense side)**: RPOs read whether second-level/run-fit defenders crash or drop — crash → the vacated zone (bubble, flat, streak behind a run-committed hook) is thrown; drop → hand off/keep. Wide formations stretch safeties apart to widen the conflict window. So repeated completions to one zone may be one defender's run/pass conflict, not a coverage hole.
- **Shell-determined edge leverage (run defense context)**: the coverage shell, not the front, staffs the run fit — cover 3 leaves the outside corner out of the fit entirely (edge outleveraged vs extra lead blockers); cover-2/Tampa-2 shells put a cloud-flat force defender on the edge; and inside-quarter safeties get aggressive fits only under **match** calls (quarters/palms/six/nine), not "cover four drop" or Tampa 2. Chart edge run-fit behavior as shell-driven.
- **Formation-shift decoys (personnel-gated)**: offense lines up in a decoy formation and shifts into the real called one at the snap — pairings are gated by exact personnel grouping, not formation name, and package tricks can smuggle players/pairings EA never intended (partially patched 2026-08-11: the chained-hot-route "conductor" speed exploit and the Hulk→spread audible are gone; natural same-personnel shifts remain). **Film trap: naive pre-snap formation charting is poisoned** — the initial alignment may be a pure decoy; chart the post-shift formation the ball is snapped from, and log decoy→real pairs as their own tendency.
