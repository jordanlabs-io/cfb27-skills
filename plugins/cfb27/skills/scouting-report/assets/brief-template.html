The Book on Deejay Lurkin
Opponent: Maryland (Donovan Rolle, PSN donrolley)|2027 fixture|Built from four games of Maryland film
He is a front-runner. Deny him the lead and he has no Plan B.

His Offense

Identity
  • Career split is roughly 65% pass. But the pass rate hides the truth:
  • The run game is his finisher, not his opener. Ahead: 26-165-3 rushing at 6.3 a carry (all three offensive TDs vs Arizona). Trailing: the run game self-deletes entirely.
  • Dressing: 11-personnel shotgun bunch and nasty sets (Bunch Open Offset, Bunch Quads, Trips-X Nasty, Wing Slot), plus a new Pistol package. Pistol Off Trips TE Cross is a starred favorite.
  • Run staples: Inside Zone from Bunch Quads Offset and Duo Over Wk (21 lifetime calls each). Speed and Triple Option are real change-ups.
  • Pass volume: Four Verticals from two formations, Deep Divide, Wildcat Flood.
  • 75% of his drops are 3-step quick game. Targets cluster short-left and deep-right.
  • Tempo is a weapon both ways: hurry-up to chase, and 15-20 play kill drives to ice leads. He uses pace to trap your substitutions.
  • 4th & short: HE GOES. Only kicks at 4th & goal. Two Arizona 4th-down stops became 14 Maryland points.

His Defense

Call sheet
  • 4-3 on 74 of 75 personnel calls. He is a 4-3 defense, full stop.
  • Cover 3 Cloud Press ×52 is THE identity call. Cover 6 ×11 is the change-up. Dime Tampa 2 shows on long downs.
  • He almost never blitzes: four-man rush on roughly 92% of snaps, zero 3rd-down blitzes on film. His sacks come from standard rushes.
  • The press is fake. Pressed cloud corners look like man every time; it is zone dressing. Trust zone-first, always.
  • Red-zone man is real. His tight goal-to-go looks produced two end-zone INTs vs UNC. Careful with goal-line throws.
  • Money-down defense wins by making you kick: Arizona went 1 of 9 on 3rd, 0 of 2 on 4th (one returned for six).

The Game Plan
Attack the cloud|Rush 4, drop 7|Get the lead, then squeeze

The Two Money Shots

vs Cover 3 Cloud Press
Smash: the hitch pins the squatting cloud corner, the corner route settles over him, under the deep third. Hole shot: a boundary vertical lives in the gold window before the half safety arrives. Your quick game and Smash family already do both.

Attack · With the Ball

Priority order
  1. Your Smash and corner-route family is the structural cloud-beater. Make it the spine of the plan (diagram above).
  2. Hunt the hole shot. Vertical at the pressed cloud corner before the half safety arrives. The same shot exists on the Cover 2 side of his Cover 6 change-up.
  3. Seams, flats, over the ball. The intermediate middle was UNC's feast zone. Put his seam-flat defender in conflict; take the checkdown when he bails.
  4. Deep shots only off play-action, when the safety spins down. His base is single-high; a naked deep-middle throw feeds the free safety.
  5. Don't get bluffed out of the quick game. The press is zone dressing. Unsure? Motion across: nickel travels means zone, safety stacking your side means man.
  6. No max protect. He rushes four and never blitzes money downs. Spend the bodies on routes; UNC took zero sacks on 43 dropbacks.
  7. Run your interior game from spread looks. Keeps the box light, and his Cover 6 change-up is run-light by design. Run right at it.
  8. Go on 4th down. A fake punt and a 4th-&-inches TD are what broke him. He has no answer script for aggression.
  9. Find #10 (Lee Jr) first. Your quick game leans left; if the pick-six corner squats there, work away or clear him out.

Stop · Without the Ball

Priority order
  1. Rush 4, drop 7 on money downs. Every UNC sack and INT came on a standard rush. Washington donates the ball when forced to convert long: 11 INTs in four games. Don't bail him out with a blitz.
  2. Spy #7 on 3rd & medium (3-6). His legs are the conversion threat there. On 3rd or 4th & long: drop seven and take the gift.
  3. Read formation weight, not bunch. Heavy means run, Empty means pass, bunch means nothing. Zero motion means the picture is honest.
  4. Screen alert on Wide Trips HB Wk. Keep the backers home; his screen trio averages 18 a pop.
  5. Wall the interior on early downs. His run game is 69% middle with no edge lean, and it is the only thing that makes him dangerous. Kill the finisher before it starts.
  6. Pick one tempo-proof personnel and live in it. He hurry-ups mid-drive specifically to trap your subs. Pre-decide your base call for tempo stretches.
  7. 4th & short: he goes. Have the stop call in your pocket before his 3rd-down snap in plus territory.

Manage · The 60 Minutes

Situational
  1. Script the start to score first. The lead decides which Maryland you play. This is the whole game.
  2. Ball security over hero throws. His defense manufactured three INTs off UNC in '26 and Lee Jr took two off Arizona. At the goal line take the field goal; his red-zone man looks eat forced throws, and he gives points back on his own money downs.
  3. If he leads in Q4, spend timeouts early in his drive. His kill drives run 15-20 plays on tempo. A stop only matters while you still have a possession left.
  4. Take the delay-of-game, keep the timeout. Five yards costs far less than the timeout you never get back.
  5. Tempo levers: normal keeps your full playbook; turbo chews clock and adds OL push but cuts you to a two-play menu. Use deliberately, not by default.
  6. Mirror his 4th-down aggression. He will go on you; his defense breaks when you go on him.

Clean Up Your Side · This Week

  1. Your blitz habit is your leak. Your pressure calls have given up chunk plays on film; your zone and contain looks have been far stingier. Against his 3-step quick game a blitz mostly donates the hot route. Coverage-first fixes both problems.
  2. Bench the PA Read this game. It hasn't paid on film. Feed Smash and the quick RPO game instead.
  3. Stay out of 3rd & long (0 for 3 on film). Early-down interior run plus the 3-step game keeps you ahead of the chains.
  4. Your under-center look screams run (8 run, 0 pass on film). Avoid it, or break it once early with play-action to poison the read.
