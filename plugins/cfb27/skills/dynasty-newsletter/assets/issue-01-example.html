For fifty-one weeks, we lived with it. We lived with 31–21. We lived with the memory of a 14–0 lead at Kenan evaporating into 28 unanswered Maryland points. We lived with the quietest walk down Franklin Street in recorded history — 50,000 people shuffling home like a funeral procession, nobody saying a word. Some of us lived with an actual Maryland fan in our Tuesday seminar who wore that pristine red jersey to class and just let it do the talking for an hour and fifteen minutes.

Saturday, in College Park, the Tar Heels took all of it — the jersey guy, the silent walk, the smirk — and fed it into a woodchipper.

North Carolina 37, Maryland 14. Nineteenth-ranked UNC against the No. 4 team in the country, in their building, in the SEC opener. The stadium's own postgame board typed the word for us: CAKEWALK.

We're back. Say it with your chest this time. We're back.

The Revenge Game
UNC 37 · Maryland 14

The Wound

You can't appreciate Saturday without last October. Both teams 4-0, both ranked, Kenan Stadium so loud on the opening touchdown and the pick-six that followed that the bleachers physically vibrated. Up 14–0 that night, we were booking imaginary playoff tickets from the student section. And then Maryland's coach — the league knows him as Deejay Lurkin, and he is exactly as cold-blooded as the name suggests — did not blink. He didn't panic. He waited. What followed was 28 unanswered points and a masterclass in watching an opponent unravel: three interceptions, four sacks, and the dagger — a fifteen-play fourth-quarter death march where everyone in the stadium knew the fourth-and-one run was coming, and it converted anyway. 31–21. Clinical. Merciless. The kind of loss that makes juniors feel like they need knee replacements.

How Both Sides Got Here

That Kenan collapse broke the 2026 season's spirit for a stretch — the Baylor shootout and the Northwestern slow-motion nightmare came right after — before the Heels steadied and won five of their last six to climb back into the Top 25 at 8-3. Maryland just kept being Maryland: they stacked wins down the stretch, rode into the top ten, and opened 2027 a perfect 3-0, ranked No. 4, looking every bit the apex predator of this league.

Rock Bottom Has a Sky Box

Here's what makes Saturday absurd: seven days earlier, this looked like the same old story. Carolina had opened 2027 with two wins and climbed to No. 10 in the country when No. 9 USC came to Kenan — a top-ten home date, Chapel Hill buzzing — and the Trojans walked out with the win, nine spots of our ranking, and most of the goodwill from the 2-0 start.

So the storyline heading to College Park was not kind: young quarterback, coming off a home gut-punch, first conference road trip, against the No. 4 team, against that coach, in that rivalry. The team apparently didn't get the memo.

A Quarter of Nerves, Then an Avalanche

For fifteen minutes it was everything we feared. Miles O'Neill fired one into the end zone that got picked at the goal line; our defense answered by intercepting Maryland's QB Washington when the Terps got greedy on third-and-inches. One quarter: 0–0, two turnovers, two fan bases chewing their nails.

Then Carolina remembered who it's supposed to be this year. The Heels converted a third-and-inches on an O'Neill keeper — he took a shot and got up smiling — then went for it on fourth-and-inches at the goal line and threw for the touchdown. No field-goal unit, no fear. With the half dying, a picture-perfect two-minute drill ended with the back nosing his way into the end zone. 14–0 at the break — the exact lead we blew last year, which nobody in Carolina blue needed reminding of.

The third quarter is where the rivalry flipped for good. Facing fourth-and-one near midfield, Carolina lined up to punt. Except — in the broadcast's own words — “the punt was just an illusion.” The snap went to the up-back, the chains moved, and on the next play Jordan Shipp hauled in a thirty-yard strike. O'Neill scrambled seventeen on third-and-long to twist the knife. The drive only produced a field goal, but the message was the point: the team that got out-nerved in this fixture last October was now calling fake punts in the other guy's stadium.

Then, avalanche. A field goal, 20–0. The deep-shot touchdown dropped into the Maryland-flag end zone, 27–0. Washington's fourth-and-forever heave intercepted. Another field goal, 30–0. Maryland finally broke the shutout with six minutes left; Carolina answered with one more touchdown anyway, set up by a deep ball that drew a flag. The late pick-six made it 37–14, and even that couldn't dent the mood. Thirty-seven unanswered points between Maryland's empty first quarter and their garbage time.

Defense & The Verdict
Money downs: 1/9 · 0/7

The Defense Deserves Its Own Newsletter

Last year Maryland's offense took our souls. This year our defense took their lunch money and made them watch. The numbers are almost rude: Maryland converted one of nine third downs and zero of seven fourth downs. Washington — the same quarterback whose legs beat us at Kenan — was picked off twice on the money downs, sacked three-plus times, and held scoreless until the game was decided. Sophomore linebacker Jonathan Agumadu lived in the backfield with a sack and a tackle for loss. Every must-have down, eleven guys in white said no.

And for everyone who watched our quarterback get planted four times in last year's meeting: O'Neill was not sacked once. Forty-plus dropbacks. Clean jersey. The offensive line won this rivalry as much as anybody.

What It Means

One September Saturday doesn't erase 31–21 — rivalries don't work like that, and Deejay Lurkin's teams don't stay down long. Maryland is still loaded, still ranked, and we get to do this dance again.

But here's what changed in College Park: this program stopped flinching. Down a ranking, coming off a home gut-punch, staring at the coach who authored our worst night in years — Carolina went for it on fourth down, faked a punt, threw deep, and hit back every single time it mattered. The team that folded at 14–0 last October led 14–0 at half and stepped on the gas.

A year ago, a podcast that sounded suspiciously like three roommates in a Franklin Street apartment spent twenty-seven minutes doing group therapy over this team and ended with a question: do we even renew our student tickets?

Consider Issue 1 our official answer. Renew. We're back.— The Tarstool Sports Desk. Fight, fight, fight.
